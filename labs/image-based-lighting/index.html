Image-based lighting lab

The light source is a picture of the whole surroundings, stored as a cube map. Every direction is a light. Adding up thousands of directions per pixel is too slow, so the sums are done ahead of time and stored in three small textures. At run time a pixel reads them and is done.

color = diffuse × irradiance(N)
      + prefiltered(R, roughness)
        × (F0 × A + B)

Try this

  1. Click through the stages under the scene, 1 to 7: the light, its two blurred versions, the table, the two terms, and their sum.
  2. Step the Specular menu from 1 to 3 and watch the rough spheres on the right. A mirror read is wrong for them, the prefiltered map fixes the blur, and the table adds the bright edges.
  3. Switch between 3. Split sum and 4. Reference. They are close, at a tiny fraction of the texture reads. The largest differences are on rough spheres near their edges.
  4. With Sunny sky, turn off Read a blurrier mip and open stage 3. The sun turns into bright dots. Raise Samples per texel and they fade slowly; the checkbox removes them at once.
  5. Pick White furnace. The light is the same from everywhere, so a perfect white object would be invisible. The rough metal spheres are darker than the background: energy is lost. Turn on Energy compensation and they vanish.
  6. Switch Diffuse between the cube map and spherical harmonics on the plastic row. Nine colors give nearly the same result as the texture.
  7. Pick Studio softboxes with the Torus knot and drag Rotate.
