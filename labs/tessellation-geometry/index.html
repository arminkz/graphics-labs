Tessellation and geometry shader lab

Both stages make new geometry on the GPU. Tessellation turns a few coarse patches into many triangles, as many as each patch needs. A geometry shader then sees one finished triangle at a time and can change it, drop it or emit extra ones.

vertex→TCS→tessellator→TES→geometry→fragment
Browsers have neither stage: WebGL 2 and WebGPU both leave them out. This lab runs the same steps by other means. JavaScript computes the levels and applies the tessellator's subdivision rules; an instanced vertex shader does the evaluation and the geometry-shader work. The code box below shows the real OpenGL 4 GLSL for whatever is selected.

Try this

      
    
        
  1. Click through the stages under the scene, 1 to 5: patches in, levels chosen, coordinates generated, vertices placed, triangles post-processed.
    2. 
        
  2. Pick Manual, set Outer to 1 and drag Inner: the borders stay coarse while the middle fills in. Then the reverse. The small diagram shows the pattern for one patch.
    3. 
        
  3. With equal_spacing, drag a level slowly: triangles pop in at whole numbers. With a fractional mode, new vertices slide out from the edge ends.
    4. 
        
  4. On the terrain, switch between per edge and per patch and look along patch borders: per patch leaves slits (pink background showing through) where two neighbors disagree about their shared edge.
    5. 
        
  5. Orbit with a distance mode on and watch the Tessellation levels stage: detail follows the camera.
    6. 
        
  6. On the Bézier scene, set both levels to 1: sixteen control points collapse to two flat triangles. Raise them and the curve appears.
    7. 
        
  7. Pick Grass and change the levels: the blade count follows the triangle count, because the geometry shader runs once per triangle.
