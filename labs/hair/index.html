Hair rendering lab

Hair is thousands of thin, shiny, translucent cylinders. That breaks three habits from surface rendering: there is no surface normal, light passes through the fibers, and the strands shadow each other like a volume.

1. Shading model

A fiber seen end-on. R bounces off the outside (white). TT goes through (bright when the light is behind). TRT bounces once inside and comes back tinted by the hair's pigment.

Try this

  1. Step through the three shading models. Surface shading gives a round blob. Kajiya–Kay turns it into a band across the strands. Marschner splits the band into a white one and a colored one.
  2. Press Highlight close-up and drag Cuticle tilt: the white (R) and colored (TRT) highlights slide apart along the strands. At 0 they sit on top of each other.
  3. Press Back-lit shot and toggle TT: the bright rim around the silhouette is light passing through the fibers. Try it with blond, then black.
  4. Set shadows to None: the hair goes flat and glows from inside. Hard is noisy. The deep approximation gives depth.
  5. Pick Blond and drag Multi-scatter to 0: light hair turns dark and dull, because most of its brightness is light bouncing between strands.
  6. Zoom far out and toggle Thin-strand fade: without it, strands stay one pixel wide and the hair looks like wire.
